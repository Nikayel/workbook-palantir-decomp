# Flashcards — Rank the Notifications Feed

> Review day 1 → 3 → 7 → 14. Reset on a miss. Free recall only.

**Q:** Why is raw CTR a dangerous success metric for notifications?
**A:** It's a vanity metric — it can rise while users get annoyed and opt out. Pair any engagement metric with a **guardrail** (opt-out rate) that can veto a launch.

**Q:** The reframe that separates a strong notifications answer from a weak one?
**A:** It's not "too many notifications," it's "too few *valuable* ones" — optimize **value per notification**, not volume.

**Q:** What single move do interviewers most often find missing in PM answers?
**A:** An **explicit cut** — naming what you will NOT build (and why), not just what you will.

**Q:** North Star vs guardrail vs counter-metric — what's each for?
**A:** North Star = the value you steer by; guardrail = a line you must not cross (can block launch); counter-metric = the way your metric might be gamed.

**Q:** "Start rules, earn ML" — what does it mean for an MVP?
**A:** Ship a rules-based version (bundle/cap obvious low-value types) to capture most value cheaply and de-risk the model, *then* invest in ML.

**Q:** A/B test essentials a PM should state?
**A:** Hypothesis, unit of randomization (user), primary metric + guardrails, and the ship/kill bar over a defined run.

**Q:** How do you evaluate a leadership-pushed "add AI" feature without sounding like a yes-person?
**A:** Define the user value first, set a guardrail, then A/B against the same North Star — ship because it moves the metric, not because it's "AI."

**Q:** Why segment before solutioning?
**A:** The bleeding segment (heavy users opting out — nearly irreversible) tells you where impact is; designing for "everyone" produces generic me-too features.

**Q:** Meta's two PM pillars?
**A:** **Product Sense** ("what would you build for billions") and **data-driven Execution** (metrics, tradeoffs, A/B) — not project management.
