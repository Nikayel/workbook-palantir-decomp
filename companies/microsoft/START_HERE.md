## Culture

Microsoft's culture runs on three principles:

**Growth Mindset ("learn-it-all" not "know-it-all").**
Satya Nadella's defining contribution to Microsoft. The single most important cultural keyword in your interview. Every story you tell should end with what you learned or how you grew — not with how impressive you already were. Interviewers are trained to probe for this.

**Model-Coach-Care.**
Microsoft managers are expected to model behavior, coach for growth, and care for individuals. Behavioral questions test whether you embody this as a peer (do you help teammates grow?).

**Respect, Integrity, Accountability.**
The three RIA values appear in every formal review cycle. In behavioral questions, they translate to: respect = how you treat people with less power, integrity = what you do when no one is watching, accountability = how you own mistakes.

## What's Distinctive About Microsoft Interviews

**Growth mindset is the single most important interview keyword.**
If you say "I already knew how to solve this" you have scored zero on the culture dimension. Every answer — even a pure coding problem — should include a moment where you say what you'd do differently, what you learned, or how you'd grow.

**Solid CS fundamentals explained well beat trick puzzles.**
Microsoft does not value esoteric algorithmic tricks. They want clean, readable code with O(n) / O(n log n) solutions explained clearly. If you can narrate your thought process and explain trade-offs, you're ahead of someone who silently writes a clever one-liner.

**Format: Codility OA + Superday.**
- Codility OA: 90 minutes, 2 problems (easy-medium algorithmic). Timed but not proctored live.
- Superday: 3-4 rounds back to back. Each round = ~15 min behavioral ("Tell me about a time...") + ~30-35 min coding or product question. Behavioral happens FIRST in each round — not at the end.

**OOP/LLD shows up more than at peer companies.**
Microsoft's engineering culture is C#/.NET-rooted. Object-oriented design problems (class hierarchies, design patterns, extensibility questions) are much more common at Microsoft than at Google or Meta. You should be comfortable with abstract base classes, interfaces, inheritance, and composition.

## Before You Start

**The one habit to build before any Microsoft lab:**

Practice framing EVERY story around learning and growth — not around already knowing the answer.

Bad framing: "I solved this quickly because I'd seen a similar problem before."
Good framing: "I hadn't seen this type of problem before. I got stuck on X. What helped me was Y. Next time I'd Z earlier."

The interviewer is not looking for someone who never makes mistakes. They're looking for someone who grows from them. That is the Microsoft growth mindset.

Practice this reframe with three stories before starting Lab 01.
