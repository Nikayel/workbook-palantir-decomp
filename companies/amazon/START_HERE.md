## The 16 Leadership Principles

Memorize all 16. In interviews, you will be expected to name them, give examples from your experience that map to specific LPs, and — for PM — make product decisions that reflect them.

| # | Leadership Principle | Core meaning |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Customer Obsession** | Start with the customer, work backwards. Customer trust over short-term revenue. |
| 2 | **Ownership** | Act on behalf of the whole company, not just your team. Long-term thinking. |
| 3 | **Invent and Simplify** | Demand innovation AND simplicity from your team. Simple is harder and better. |
| 4 | **Are Right, A Lot** | Strong judgment and good instincts. Seek diverse perspectives to challenge your view. |
| 5 | **Learn and Be Curious** | Never stop learning. Curious about new possibilities. |
| 6 | **Hire and Develop the Best** | Recognize exceptional talent; raise the performance bar. |
| 7 | **Insist on the Highest Standards** | Relentlessly high standards — many think they're unreasonably high. Fix problems. |
| 8 | **Think Big** | Bold direction, inspire results. Think differently. Small thinking is self-fulfilling prophecy. |
| 9 | **Bias for Action** | Speed matters. Calculated risks. Many decisions are reversible — don't over-process. |
| 10 | **Frugality** | Accomplish more with less. Constraints breed resourcefulness. |
| 11 | **Earn Trust** | Listen, speak candidly, be self-critical. Benchmark against the best. |
| 12 | **Dive Deep** | Stay connected to details. Audit frequently. Be skeptical when metrics differ from anecdote. |
| 13 | **Have Backbone; Disagree and Commit** | Respectfully challenge decisions you disagree with. Commit fully once decided. |
| 14 | **Deliver Results** | Focus on the key inputs and deliver with the right quality and timeliness. |
| 15 | **Strive to be Earth's Best Employer** | Work to be the safest, most productive, most effective, and most fun. |
| 16 | **Success and Scale Bring Broad Responsibility** | Humble about the impact. Make good choices for customers, employees, communities. |

### Most Interview-Load-Bearing LPs

These are the LPs that come up most frequently in behavioral questions and that interviewers are most actively looking for:

| LP | Why it's central | Common question form |
|---|---|---|
| **Customer Obsession** | #1 LP. All decisions start here. | "Tell me about a time you went above and beyond for a customer." |
| **Ownership** | Differentiates leaders from executors. | "Tell me about a time you took ownership of a problem that wasn't yours." |
| **Dive Deep** | Separates rigorous thinkers from hand-wavers. | "Tell me about a time you used data to challenge an assumption." |
| **Invent and Simplify** | Shows creative problem-solving AND restraint. | "Tell me about a time you found a simpler solution to a complex problem." |
| **Bias for Action** | Tests whether you act or wait for permission. | "Tell me about a time you made a decision with incomplete information." |
| **Are Right, A Lot** | Tests judgment quality. | "Tell me about a time you were wrong. How did you know?" |
| **Earn Trust** | Tests interpersonal credibility. | "Tell me about a time you had a difficult conversation with a colleague." |
| **Have Backbone** | Tests willingness to disagree up. | "Tell me about a time you pushed back on a leader's decision." |
| **Deliver Results** | The floor — if you can't deliver, LPs are moot. | "Tell me about your most significant professional accomplishment." |

## What Makes Amazon Distinctive

### LP-Mapped STAR is the #1 Differentiator

Even for SWE roles, the behavioral component is weighted heavily. Many candidates fail not because they can't code, but because their behavioral answers are generic ("I worked on a team that built X") rather than LP-anchored ("I demonstrated Ownership by...").

**For SWE:** After solving a problem, you may be asked "Walk me through how you would communicate this solution to a non-technical stakeholder" (Earn Trust) or "What would you do if you discovered a critical bug 30 minutes before launch?" (Bias for Action + Ownership).

**The Bar Raiser** is an interviewer trained to hold the bar independently of the hiring team. They have veto power. They are specifically looking for LP signals that other interviewers might miss, and for evidence that you will raise the bar — not just meet it.

### Writing Culture

Amazon is famous for its writing culture. Meetings start with silent reading of a 6-page document. For PM roles, the PR-FAQ (Press Release + Frequently Asked Questions) is the primary artifact of the Working Backwards process. Knowing how to write — clearly, concisely, with customer-first framing — is a core competency, not just a nice-to-have.

**6-pager format:** Narrative (no slides). Problem, solution, impact, risks, open questions. Dense with data. Starts with the customer's problem.

**PR-FAQ format:** Press release written as if the product is already launched (future state, customer benefit), followed by FAQ. Working Backwards means the PR-FAQ is written before a single line of code.

### Bar Raiser Veto

The Bar Raiser is a certified interviewer (separate from the hiring team) whose job is to independently assess whether the candidate raises the bar for the company. If the Bar Raiser says "no," the candidate does not get an offer — regardless of what the rest of the loop thinks.

The Bar Raiser looks for LP evidence that is specific, quantified, and recent. "I worked with a team" is not evidence of Ownership. "I identified the risk, escalated with a written memo, and stayed through the weekend to fix the production issue" is.

## Assessment Formats

### SWE OA

| Component | Format | What's tested |
|---|---|---|
| Coding problems | 2 medium LeetCode problems, ~70 min | Technical correctness, edge cases |
| Work Simulation | Inbox triage — respond to 3-5 emails/scenarios | LP alignment in decision-making |
| Work Style Assessment | ~15 min self-report | Personality/work preferences (used for calibration, not scored pass/fail) |

**Work Simulation scoring:** Each inbox scenario maps to 1-2 LPs. Your responses are scored on LP alignment. There is no trick — the right answer is the one that most clearly demonstrates Customer Obsession, Ownership, or the LP that scenario tests. Know the LPs before starting the WS.

### PM OA / Interview Loop

| Round | Format | LPs tested |
|---|---|---|
| OA (if applicable) | Product sense written or WS | Customer Obsession, Invent and Simplify |
| PR-FAQ writing | 60 min in-interview | Working Backwards, Customer Obsession, Are Right A Lot |
| Product case | 45 min | Dive Deep (metrics), Invent and Simplify, Think Big |
| LP behavioral | 45 min | All — especially Have Backbone, Earn Trust, Deliver Results |
| Bar Raiser | Variable | Highest LP bar; often combines product + behavioral |

## Before You Start

**Step 1: Memorize the 16 LPs.** Not just the names — know each one's core meaning in one sentence. Take a sheet of paper, write all 16 from memory, then check yourself. Do this three times before your first lab.

**Step 2: Build a STAR bank of 10–12 stories.** Each story should be usable for multiple LPs. Aim for stories that are: specific (named project, named outcome), quantified (metrics, time, scale), and recent (< 3 years). Cover both wins AND failures — Amazon explicitly asks about failures.

**STAR format:**
- Situation: context in 1–2 sentences
- Task: what you were responsible for
- Action: what YOU specifically did (not "we")
- Result: quantified outcome + what you learned

**Step 3: Practice PR-FAQ format (PM).** Write one PR-FAQ before your first lab. Use an Amazon product you know well (Alexa, Prime, AWS). Time yourself — 60 minutes. Then compare to Amazon's public documentation on Working Backwards.

**Step 4: Know the Bar Raiser.** In your final loop, one interviewer is the Bar Raiser. You will not be told which one. Treat every interviewer as if they are the Bar Raiser. Your LP answers should be specific, evidence-based, and recent in every round.
