# Meta PM Lab 02 — Jedi Behavioral
## The 5-Dimension Behavioral Round (Tier 2)

**Tier:** 2 | **Role:** PM | **Est. time:** 45 min | **Difficulty:** Medium

**Before you start:** Set a timer for 45 minutes. This lab simulates the Meta "Jedi" behavioral round. Unlike Amazon STAR (one story, minimal follow-up), Meta interviewers follow up 3-5 times on each story. A story that can't sustain 4 follow-ups is not ready for the Jedi round.

---

- [ ] M1 · 5 dimensions mapped — can explain what each Jedi dimension means in concrete behavior (not definitions)
- [ ] M2 · Stories prepared — at least one STAR story per dimension
- [ ] M3 · Follow-up ready — each story can sustain 4 follow-ups before running dry
- [ ] M4 · Values woven in — stories reference Meta values ("Move Fast", "Be Direct", "Long-Term Impact", etc.)
- [ ] M5 · Defended — handled the tough curveball follow-ups
- [ ] M6 · Ready — self-graded ≥ 28/35

## Part 0 — Forethought

**Scenario:** Your Meta PM/RPM Jedi round interviewer says:

> "I'm going to ask you about a few situations that reveal how you work. I want you to tell me a real story for each, and then I'll follow up to understand it more deeply. Let's start with a time you had to resolve a significant conflict."

**What makes this different from Amazon behavioral:**

| Amazon (STAR) | Meta (Jedi) |
|---|---|
| One story, well-structured | One story, sustained depth |
| Interviewer moves on after your answer | Interviewer follows up 3-5 times |
| Stories tested across 14+ LPs | Stories tested across 5 Jedi dimensions |
| Breadth across your career | Depth on one or two pivotal moments |
| "Tell me about a time..." | "Tell me about a time..." → "Why?" → "What did they say?" → "What would you do differently?" → "How did it affect the team after?" |

**Meta Jedi evaluation:** The interviewer is looking for whether your story holds up at depth. A great STAR story that runs out of detail at the second follow-up fails the Jedi round. A less polished story that reveals genuine learning and self-awareness at the fifth follow-up passes.

**Target time:** 45 minutes. Suggested breakdown:
- 5 min — map 5 Jedi dimensions to concrete behaviors (Part 1)
- 30 min — write 5 stories (Part 2), 6 min each
- 5 min — Meta framing (Part 3)
- 5 min — curveballs (Part 6)

## Part 1 — Jedi Dimensions: Concrete Behaviors

*Don't write definitions. Write what the dimension looks like in observable behavior — what a colleague would SEE you doing.*

**Dimension 1: Resolve Conflict**

What it looks like: [blank]

*Model:* Didn't triangulate (didn't go to the manager first). Went directly to the other person within 24 hours. Named the disagreement explicitly rather than working around it. Found the underlying interest (not position) that each party had. Landed on a resolution both could articulate to a third party. Didn't need the conflict to be fully resolved before working together again — was willing to tolerate disagreement on one issue while continuing to collaborate on others.

---

**Dimension 2: Grow Continuously**

What it looks like: [blank]

*Model:* Proactively asked for feedback without prompting ("what's one thing I could have done better?"). Received critical feedback without defensiveness — paused, asked clarifying questions, said "that's helpful" before responding. Changed observable behavior based on the feedback within the next project. Sought out a role, project, or skill that was outside their comfort zone. Can name 3 specific things they've gotten better at in the last 18 months.

---

**Dimension 3: Embrace Ambiguity**

What it looks like: [blank]

*Model:* Started with partial information and stated assumptions explicitly rather than waiting for clarity. Made a decision and moved, with an explicit plan to revisit if the assumption turned out to be wrong. Didn't ask the manager to define the problem before starting — defined it themselves and proposed a path. When new information arrived that invalidated an assumption, updated the plan without frustration. Named what they didn't know as clearly as what they did know.

---

**Dimension 4: Drive Results**

What it looks like: [blank]

*Model:* Defined what success looked like BEFORE starting — in a metric, not a vague goal. Tracked the metric weekly and reported it to stakeholders unprompted. When the metric wasn't moving, changed approach rather than explaining why it wasn't moving. Delivered the outcome even when the process changed mid-way. Can quantify the impact: "we shipped 3 weeks early" or "NPS increased by 12 points" or "retention improved 8%."

---

**Dimension 5: Communicate Effectively**

What it looks like: [blank]

*Model:* Tailored the message to the audience without prompting — technical depth with engineers, business impact with executives, user stories with design. Delivered a difficult message directly rather than softening it to the point of losing the meaning. Followed up verbal communication with written confirmation ("just to confirm, here's what we agreed to"). When there was disagreement after a communication, asked "what did you hear me say?" rather than repeating the same message louder. Was told by stakeholders that they "always know where things stand."

## Part 3 — Meta-Specific Framing

**"Be Direct" in conflict — what does it look like vs. being aggressive?**

[blank — your answer]

*What to address:* "Be Direct" means: (a) naming the problem explicitly without softening it to the point of losing the meaning, (b) giving the other person the full information they need to understand your perspective, (c) doing this in private first before escalating. Being aggressive means: (a) naming the problem in a way that attacks the person rather than the issue, (b) using emotional intensity to win the argument, (c) not leaving space for the other person to respond. The litmus test: after a direct conversation, both parties know what was said and can act on it. After an aggressive one, one party is demoralized.

**"Move Fast" in ambiguity — how do you move fast without making wrong assumptions?**

[blank — your answer]

*What to address:* Moving fast with ambiguity doesn't mean ignoring the unknown — it means naming the unknown explicitly ("I'm going to proceed on the assumption that X, and I'll check back in 1 week to see if X held"), deciding which assumptions are reversible (low cost to be wrong) vs. irreversible (high cost to be wrong), and investing in making the high-cost assumptions verifiable as fast as possible. "Move Fast" fails when you make high-cost irreversible assumptions without knowing they're high-cost.

**"Long-Term Impact" vs "Drive Results" — when does a short-term result hurt long-term?**

[blank — your answer]

*What to address:* A short-term metric win can damage long-term impact when: (a) you optimize for a leading indicator that diverges from the outcome (e.g., you ship more features, but each feature is low quality and increases technical debt), (b) you drive engagement in a way that burns trust (e.g., a notification campaign that drives short-term opens but long-term opt-outs), (c) you hit the quarterly goal by pulling forward demand that hurts next quarter. "Drive Results" at Meta means driving the right results at the right time horizon — not just hitting this quarter's number.

## Part 6 — Jedi Follow-Up Simulation (Curveballs)

### Curveball 1

**Interviewer:** "Tell me about a time your recommendation was wrong. How did you handle being wrong?"

**Your answer:** [blank]

*Things to address:*
- This tests intellectual honesty and the Grow Continuously dimension simultaneously.
- The strongest answers: (a) name a real wrong recommendation (not "I thought the launch should be later, and it turned out I was right" — the opposite direction is needed), (b) describe how you found out you were wrong (your own discovery vs. someone else pointing it out — discovering your own errors scores higher), (c) name what you changed after being wrong, (d) describe how you communicated the correction to stakeholders ("I was wrong about X, here's what I know now, here's what I'm changing").
- What to avoid: "I was wrong about a minor detail" (not high-stakes enough), "I've been pretty lucky in my recommendations" (not credible), claiming a wrong recommendation you then fixed before anyone noticed doesn't count for this question — they want to know how you HANDLED being wrong, which requires others knowing you were wrong.

---

### Curveball 2

**Interviewer:** "Give me an example where you had to communicate a difficult message to a senior stakeholder."

**Your answer:** [blank]

*Things to address:*
- This tests the Communicate Effectively dimension with a power dynamic added.
- Strong story elements: (a) the message had real stakes (not "I told my manager I'd be 1 day late"), (b) you were direct — you said the thing that was uncomfortable to say, (c) you prepared for the reaction and had data ready, (d) the senior stakeholder didn't shoot the messenger — or if they did, you held your position with data.
- The failure mode: softening the message so much that the stakeholder didn't receive it. "I kind of suggested we might want to reconsider the timeline" is not direct communication. The Jedi dimension is "Be Direct" — the message must land.

---

### Curveball 3

**Interviewer:** "Tell me about a time you failed to deliver results. What happened and what did you learn?"

**Your answer:** [blank]

*Things to address:*
- This tests the Drive Results dimension from failure, plus Grow Continuously.
- The strongest answers: (a) name a real failure where a goal was missed, not a near-miss, (b) take personal ownership — not "the team missed the goal" but "I missed the goal" and then explain why, (c) distinguish between what you could and couldn't control — and name what you should have done differently within what you could control, (d) name the specific lesson and how it changed a subsequent project.
- The failure mode candidates most often make: attributing the failure entirely to external factors ("the market changed," "the engineering resources were reallocated") with no personal ownership. At Meta, "Drive Results" means you find a way even when the path is blocked. If you didn't find a way, what would you do differently next time to find it?
